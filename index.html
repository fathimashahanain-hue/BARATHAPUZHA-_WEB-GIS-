About Bharathapuzha

The lifeline of the Malabar region

Bharathapuzha, popularly known as Nila, is the second-longest river in Kerala after the Periyar. Originating in the Anaimalai Hills of the Western Ghats, the river flows westward through Tamil Nadu and Kerala before draining into the Arabian Sea at Ponnani.


The river basin extends across Palakkad, Thrissur and Malappuram districts of Kerala and parts of Tamil Nadu. It plays an important role in agriculture, irrigation, settlement development, biodiversity and the cultural landscape of the region.


Bharathapuzha is not merely a river system; it is an important geographical and cultural landscape that supports human and ecological systems.

Major Tributaries

The important tributaries include Gayathripuzha, Kannadipuzha (Chitturpuzha), Kalpathipuzha and Thuthapuzha.


Geographical Significance

• Supports agricultural activities and irrigation.
• Influences settlement patterns and regional development.
• Provides freshwater and ecological habitats.
• Contributes to the cultural heritage of Kerala.
• Maintains important riverine and riparian ecosystems.
